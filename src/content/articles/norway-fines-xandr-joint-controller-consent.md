The Norwegian Data Protection Authority has fined Xandr, an advertising technology company now part of Microsoft, **NOK 92 million (about EUR 8.6 million)**. The case began with a 2020 complaint by the Norwegian Consumer Council, supported by noyb, about real-time bidding in the dating app Grindr.

The regulator found that Xandr processed Grindr users' advertising IDs, IP addresses and the fact that they used the app. Because the app is aimed at gay, bi, trans and queer people, this is data concerning sexual orientation under Article 9 GDPR.

Xandr was a joint controller and did not obtain consent itself, relying instead on Grindr's consent — which was invalid. The decision states plainly that **accountability cannot be outsourced**: each joint controller must check that consent is valid and covers its own processing. The infringements were judged at least negligent and of high seriousness.

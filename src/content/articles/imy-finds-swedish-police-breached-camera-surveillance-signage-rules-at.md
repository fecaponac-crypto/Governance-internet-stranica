Sweden's data protection authority, IMY, has found that the **Swedish Police Authority breached the Camera Surveillance Act** at Gothenburg Central Station. Its review found that the police had not met the requirement to inform people clearly, for example through signs, in direct connection with the monitored area.

The review followed a complaint and was limited to the Act's information requirement. Under that requirement, it must be clear which area is under surveillance. As a rule, people should also be informed before they enter it.

In its decision, IMY identifies places where more signs are needed. In particular, there was **no clear signage for people arriving at the station by train**, which IMY says must be corrected. IMY's announcement does not mention a fine.

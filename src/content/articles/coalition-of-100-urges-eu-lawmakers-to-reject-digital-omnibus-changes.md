European Digital Rights (EDRi) reports that **100 organisations and individuals**, including civil society groups, academics, researchers and what it calls responsible businesses, have signed an open letter to EU lawmakers on the Digital Omnibus. The European Commission presented the package on 19 November 2025 as a simplification exercise.

The signatories argue that simplification is becoming deregulation. They object in particular to a proposed route under the GDPR allowing AI development, training and operation to rely on **legitimate interest**. EDRi says existing legal tests would remain but warns that large-scale reuse of personal data for AI could become normalised. Other proposed changes cited cover sensitive and biometric data, automated decision-making, access and transparency rights, and device confidentiality under ePrivacy.

The letter asks the European Parliament and member states to reject provisions that weaken rights. It also asks them to address any genuine problems through evidence, consultation and scrutiny.

*Editor's note: This article relies solely on a report by EDRi, a digital rights advocacy group that is part of the coalition behind the letter.*

The US Federal Trade Commission has proposed an enforcement policy statement on **personalised pricing**, defined as using personal data to set prices according to what a company believes an individual will pay. The draft says that undisclosed collection or use of personal data for this purpose could be an unfair or deceptive practice under the FTC Act. The Commission voted 2-0 to publish it for comment.

According to Covington's Inside Privacy, the draft drew **more than 4,500 comments**, many questioning whether it separates individualised price increases from discounts and loyalty programmes. On 28 October 2026 the FTC also announced a market study on whether firms use personal data to set pricing and pay.

The statement is not final. Meanwhile New Jersey, Maryland and Seattle have sector-specific bans, New York requires disclosure, and Connecticut combines a ban with a mandated disclosure where exceptions apply.

Three US states have enacted laws restricting the use of personal data to set personalised prices, according to an analysis by law firm Sidley Austin. **Maryland's Protection from Predatory Pricing Act took effect on 1 October 2026**, covering food retailers and third-party delivery providers. Connecticut's surveillance pricing provisions apply from 1 July 2027, and New Jersey's Fair Price Protection Act, which **includes a private right of action**, from 1 August 2027.

The laws diverge. Maryland allows personalised prices where consumers agree to share data; Connecticut and New York's bill have no comparable consent exception. Connecticut permits non-retail sellers to use the practice if they disclose that a price was increased using personal data. Seattle's ordinance applies from 1 September 2027.

New York's One Fair Price Act, a broad ban, awaits the governor's action. The FTC proposed an enforcement policy statement in August 2026; no final version has been issued.

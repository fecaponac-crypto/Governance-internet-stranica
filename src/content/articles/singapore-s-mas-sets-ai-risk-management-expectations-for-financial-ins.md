The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) issued its Guidelines on Artificial Intelligence Risk Management for Financial Institutions on 7 October, according to a contributed analysis by Hogan Lovells partner Charmian Aw published by the IAPP. The guidelines set out supervisory expectations for governing, developing, deploying and monitoring AI.

The definition of AI is broad. It covers machine learning, foundation models, large language models, generative AI and AI agents, while deterministic rule-based automation generally falls outside scope. Low-impact use cases may follow simplified procedures. Institutions must still keep an **AI inventory**, define permitted and prohibited uses and carry out periodic reviews. Accountability rests with senior management, and outsourcing to vendors does not reduce the institution's responsibility.

Governance and oversight requirements apply from **7 October 2027**. Life cycle and capability requirements follow from 7 October 2028. These cover data management, explainability, human oversight, testing, cybersecurity, monitoring and decommissioning.

*Editor's note: This article relies on a contributed opinion piece by a law firm partner published by the IAPP, not on the MAS document itself.*

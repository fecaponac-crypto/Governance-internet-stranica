Staff at the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) have prepared draft amendments to the CCPA regulations on opt-out preference signals. The draft names **Global Privacy Control (GPC)** as an example of a commonly recognised signal that businesses selling or sharing personal information must honour. It also states that browser features acting for consumers that make personal information available must not "prevent, interfere with, contradict, or disable" the signal.

Annual metrics reporting, now limited to businesses handling data on 10,000,000 or more consumers, would extend to **any business required to complete a cybersecurity audit or risk assessment**. Covered businesses would also report opt-out requests received through a signal separately. According to Inside Privacy, the Agency cites enforcement experience showing many businesses fail to detect or honour signals.

Staff recommend that the Board start formal rulemaking with a 45-day comment period. Hearing dates have not yet been set.

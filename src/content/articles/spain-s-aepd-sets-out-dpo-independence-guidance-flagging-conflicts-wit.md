Spain's data protection authority, the AEPD, published guidance on 19 November 2026 on the statute of the data protection officer (DPO). It brings together the agency's criteria on appointment, independence, reporting to senior management, protection against dismissal and conflicts of interest. The AEPD says the document **creates no new obligations**. It interprets GDPR Articles 37 to 39 and Articles 34 to 37 of the LOPDGDD in light of CJEU rulings and a 2026 judgment of Spain's Audiencia Nacional.

The guidance treats independence as functional, hierarchical, economic and organisational. It advises against variable pay that could affect the DPO's objectivity. It reviews conflicts case by case, with no automatic incompatibilities. Senior managers with operational duties, information security leads, whistleblowing channel managers and **data or AI leads with decision-making power** may face conflicts. Legal, compliance, internal audit and transparency roles may be compatible if they do not decide purposes and means.

The AEPD plans to add good practice on resolving conflicts and an analysis of how DPO duties relate to AI responsibilities.

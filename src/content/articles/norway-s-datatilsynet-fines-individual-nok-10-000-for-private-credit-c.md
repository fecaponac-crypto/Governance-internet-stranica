Norway's data protection authority, Datatilsynet, has fined a private individual **NOK 10,000** for processing personal data without a legal basis under the GDPR.

The case began with a complaint from someone who had been credit-checked by the individual. According to Datatilsynet, the individual had access to a credit information service through their business but used it to run a credit check on the complainant for private purposes. Information from that check was then published in several Facebook groups.

Datatilsynet concluded that the check was unrelated to the individual's business activity. It found that **neither the credit check nor the publication had a legal basis**. The authority said it received no appeal within the appeal deadline, so the decision stands.

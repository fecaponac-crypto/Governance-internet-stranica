The UK Information Commissioner's Office published brief guidance on 11 November 2026 on subject access requests (SARs) generated or drafted with AI, covering requests under **Article 15 of the UK GDPR**.

The ICO's core position is that using AI does not make a request invalid; what counts is whether the person is clearly asking for their personal information. Inaccurate legal references, foreign laws or non-existent case law do not make a SAR invalid, and a request is not complex simply because it is lengthy, unclear or includes legal references. The usual tools apply: clarification where reasonably required (which pauses the clock), an extension of **up to two further months** for genuinely complex requests, and refusal or a fee if a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive.

The guidance warns that AI may be used to fake ID or letters of authority, and asks organisations to check correspondence addresses against their records. Requests for specific information such as metadata must be considered, and metadata that identifies the person falls within the SAR's scope. Elements outside the scope of a SAR need no response under the UK GDPR.

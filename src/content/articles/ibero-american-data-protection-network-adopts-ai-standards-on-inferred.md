The Ibero-American Data Protection Network (RIPD), whose permanent secretariat is held by Spain's AEPD, has **unanimously approved** standards on using personal data in AI systems. According to the AEPD, they are the first international instrument to address the topic comprehensively in articulated provisions. They create no new general obligations but explain how existing data protection rules apply to AI.

Key points: inferences, predictions and profiles can be personal data, and inferring health, ideology or sexual orientation triggers sensitive-data protection. **Public availability alone does not permit use for training**; a legal basis and people's reasonable expectations are required. Merely stating that AI is used does not meet transparency duties. A reviewer who simply endorses an AI proposal does not provide effective human intervention. Where data cannot be fully erased from a model, organisations must justify this and exclude it from future training.

The EU-funded text also covers generative and agentic AI, biometrics, emotion recognition and neurodata.

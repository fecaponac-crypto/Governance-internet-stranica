The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) has adopted the **final version of its guidelines on legitimate interest** as a legal basis under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. Version 2.0 of Guidelines 1/2024 is dated 10 November 2026 and replaces the consultation version of 8 October 2024. It comes with an annex: a flowchart for the legitimate interest assessment.

The guidelines set out **three cumulative conditions**: a legitimate interest pursued by the controller or a third party, the need to process personal data for that interest, and a balancing test showing that the data subject's interests and rights do not take precedence. Only interests that are "lawful, clearly articulated and present and effective" count. If the aim can reasonably be achieved just as effectively by less restrictive means, the legal basis is not available. Controllers should assess and document all three conditions **before** the processing starts. The board says legitimate interest should neither be a "last resort" nor be chosen because it is seen as less constraining than other legal bases.

The EDPB's consultation report counts 80 contributions, most from business associations and companies. Many criticised the approach as too restrictive; the board says most of these observations rested on misunderstandings and "have therefore not led to significant changes", while it added clarifications, for example on when a separate assessment is needed. Requests for sector-specific guidance, including on AI, were not taken up as outside the scope.

Sweden's IMY, which says it acted as co-rapporteur, reported the adoption on 16 November. The same plenary also [chose automated decision-making in recruitment](/news/edpb-picks-automated-decision-making-in-recruitment-as-2027-coordinate/) as the board's 2027 coordinated action and [finalised its processor BCR recommendations](/news/edpb-backs-south-korea-adequacy-after-first-review-and-finalises-proce/).

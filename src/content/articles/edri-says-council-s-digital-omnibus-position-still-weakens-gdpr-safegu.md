European Digital Rights (EDRi) has published a critique of the negotiating position on the Digital Omnibus that EU governments agreed on **21 October 2026**. According to EDRi, the Council corrected some of the Commission's proposals but kept changes that ease data reuse and make redress harder.

EDRi objects to Article 88bis, which recognises legitimate interest as a basis for developing or operating AI systems, and a proposed exception allowing incidental sensitive data in AI datasets to be retained where removal is technically impossible. It also criticises Article 25a, under which pseudonymised data could be treated as non-personal for recipients unable to identify individuals, and new ePrivacy consent exemptions for audience and contextual advertising measurement.

EDRi welcomes the Council keeping device-access rules in the ePrivacy Directive and rejecting a rewrite of the personal data definition. Negotiations now move to Parliament, where **more than 1,800 amendments** have been tabled.

The US Department of Justice's Executive Office for U.S. Trustees has asked the bankruptcy court overseeing Character.AI to appoint a **consumer privacy ombudsman** to advise on what user data may be sold and on what terms, according to The Record. The court can restrict or block such a sale.

The filing says the platform has **12 million monthly active users**, who spend about two hours a day on it on average. It contrasts the chatbot's assurances that conversations stay confidential with a privacy policy that allows disclosure of personal data and its transfer if the company is sold. The policy was updated on 1 July while the company was 'actively seeking to sell its assets'.

The office wants Character.AI to document earlier versions of the policy and how changes were communicated. Character.AI did not respond to The Record.

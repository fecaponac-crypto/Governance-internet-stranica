DIGITALEUROPE, the Brussels-based technology industry association, has set out its position on the proposed **Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA)**. It argues that the strictest sovereignty requirements should apply only to clearly defined, highly sensitive public cloud and AI workloads, such as defence and justice. Applying them to the entire public sector would, it says, raise costs without improving security.

The group also calls for one consistent European framework instead of 27 national approaches, aligned with existing cybersecurity rules. Citing a Commission estimate of **€2 million in implementation costs per digital service**, it calculates that a company with 20 services would face €40 million in the first year, plus up to €500,000 a year for maintenance and audits.

DIGITALEUROPE further asks that the next EU long-term budget allocate 25% to digital and that 25% of public procurement be opened to SMEs and scale-ups.

*Editor's note: This article relies solely on a position statement from DIGITALEUROPE, an industry advocacy group.*

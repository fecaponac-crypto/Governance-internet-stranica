Norway's data protection authority, Datatilsynet, has published guidance on the privacy and security risks of AI agents that can retrieve information, update business systems and carry out routine tasks on their own. It accompanies a talk by Eirik Gulbrandsen and Sara Rossebø on what they call **"digital ghosts"**.

The authority describes a scenario in which an employee builds an automated agent workflow against a business system, then changes role or leaves. The automation may keep running, querying the system and processing personal data, with no clear owner. Datatilsynet notes that such shadow AI can also sit inside already approved software, and that risks grow when several agents interact in "agent swarms".

Its recommendation is a systematic overview of agents, stating that **an AI actor needs a defined identity, clear access boundaries and clear accountability**. The authority stresses that the aim is not to ban AI.

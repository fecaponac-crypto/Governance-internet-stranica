The Global Privacy Assembly (GPA), which brings together more than 130 data protection authorities from over 80 countries, adopted **five resolutions** and a 2027–2030 strategic plan at its 48th annual meeting in Brussels on 16–18 November 2026, according to the CNIL.

The resolution on privacy-enhancing technologies (PETs), sponsored by Morocco's CNDP and co-sponsored by authorities including the CNIL, the ICO and Canada's OPC, urges organisations to consider PETs as part of privacy by design and by default, **proportionate to risk**. It says PETs must come with governance, training, documentation, testing, monitoring and audit, and are no substitute for legal compliance. The US Federal Trade Commission abstained.

Other resolutions cover AI in education, agentic AI (stressing that data protection duties apply and human oversight matters), data protection in anti-money-laundering partnerships, and a members' Practice Hub. The 2027 assembly will be held in Nairobi.

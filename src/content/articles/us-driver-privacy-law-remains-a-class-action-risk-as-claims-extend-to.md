Law firm Squire Patton Boggs has published an analysis arguing that the US **Driver's Privacy Protection Act of 1994 (DPPA)** remains a live litigation risk for any business that obtains, uses or rediscloses personal information derived from state motor vehicle records, not only for DMVs themselves.

The statute lets a court award **at least $2,500 in liquidated damages**, which the authors say can be recovered without proof of actual harm and makes the law attractive for class actions. According to the analysis, claims have moved beyond marketing into data breaches, where courts have split on whether a firm knowingly disclosed data, and into claims that platforms obtained tracking identifiers from DMV websites, where courts have been sceptical. The newest wave targets parking operators using licence plate readers and broker DMV lookups; federal courts in Florida let such claims survive dismissal in 2025 and 2026.

Resellers must confirm each recipient's permitted use and, unless they received the data under subsection (b)(11), keep redisclosure records for five years.

*Editor's note: The litigation trends come from commentary by a law firm that defends DPPA claims; the damages and record-keeping rules are checked against the statute.*

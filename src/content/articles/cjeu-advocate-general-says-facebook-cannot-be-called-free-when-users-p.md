Advocate General Laila Medina has advised the Court of Justice that **disclosing personal data and consenting to its commercial use is a form of payment** under the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive. The case, C-643/25, concerns the statement 'Facebook is and remains free' shown on Meta's registration page in 2015, challenged by the German consumer federation Verbraucherzentrale Bundesverband. German lower courts had limited 'free' claims to financial costs; the Bundesgerichtshof referred the question.

In her view, a service cannot be labelled 'free' where data is required for commercial use, unless it is processed **only to deliver the service or meet legal obligations**. She rejected Meta's argument that the GDPR displaces consumer law, saying both apply cumulatively.

The opinion does not bind the Court. According to the Court, the judges are now beginning their deliberations, and judgment will be given at a later date.

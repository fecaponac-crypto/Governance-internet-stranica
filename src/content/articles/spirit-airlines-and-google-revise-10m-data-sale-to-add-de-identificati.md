Spirit Airlines and Google have revised the terms of the proposed **$10 million sale** of the bankrupt airline's internal data, according to Skift, which cites a 157-page court filing released on Monday night. Google still pays $10 million but now also covers de-identification, with Tonic AI removing personal information on employees and customers before transfer. Medical examinations, payroll, drug testing, crew pairings, biometrics and union grievance filings are excluded, and Google must publicly commit not to knowingly re-identify the data.

The package reportedly includes about 1.9 billion emails, 664 million Teams items, 91 million SharePoint/OneDrive files and 30 million lines of code, which Google plans to use for its AI models. **A hearing on the sale is set for 17 November.**

WIRED had reported that the sale was on hold after the flight attendants' union objected; EPIC filed an amicus brief, arguing that anonymisation may not fully remove privacy risks in unstructured messages.

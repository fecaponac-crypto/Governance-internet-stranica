The **National Governors Association (NGA)** has published non-binding principles for AI governance. They were drawn up by its bipartisan Governors Council to Secure American AI Dominance, led by Governors Wes Moore (Maryland), Mike Braun (Indiana), Josh Stein (North Carolina) and Spencer Cox (Utah). The NGA says they are meant to inform the incoming Congress as it develops a federal AI framework.

The core principle is a division of roles. The **federal government would address national and international risks as AI is developed**, while states keep flexibility to govern AI as it is deployed. Other principles call for proportionate, evidence-based safeguards, fewer conflicting requirements and rules that do not favour larger companies.

According to CDO Magazine, the principles also call for independent evaluations of frontier models and mandatory reporting of significant safety incidents. States would retain consumer protection, privacy, discrimination and consequential decisions in housing, employment, lending and insurance. The magazine also reports a call for regular algorithm monitoring.

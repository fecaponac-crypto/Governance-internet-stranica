Meta has removed language from its commercial terms stating that it may use content from listed domains, web pages or URLs to "provide, develop, improve, research, train" its products, The Verge reports.

Critics, including Cornell law professor James Grimmelmann and Nieman Lab, had read the clause as an attempt to obtain rights to use scraped website content — including news sites — for AI training and to supersede blocking instructions in sites' robots.txt files.

Meta initially said there was no change to how it uses web crawlers. In its updated statement, the company said the consolidated crawling terms applied only to its ads and business products, that content owners can continue to use robots.txt to signal their preferences, and that the update gives it no new rights that supersede them.

General Motors will pay **$220 million** to settle allegations by a coalition of 36 attorneys general, co-led by Washington and Oregon, that it sold drivers' behaviour and location data to data brokers, who passed it to insurers. According to the Washington Attorney General, insurers used the data in part to set rates.

The investigation found that GM enrolled millions of drivers in its Smart Driver programme between 2016 and 2024, often at dealerships through a combined OnStar sign-up. GM sold the data under contracts specifying insurance use, without telling drivers. The OnStar app wrongly told **over 370,000 drivers** they were not enrolled; GM later unenrolled them quietly and left their data with brokers and insurers.

GM must now obtain affirmative express consent, limit collection, run a comprehensive privacy programme with internal and external assessments, and train dealership staff. A separate Texas lawsuit is ongoing.

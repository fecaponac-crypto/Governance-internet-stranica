The Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) has published 'Asistentes y agentes de IA: Decálogo para personas usuarias', guidance on using AI assistants and agents that act on a person's behalf. It proposes **five questions before delegating**: what the tool can see, what it can remember, what it can do on the user's behalf, where it sends data and whether it affects others.

The AEPD advises granting only essential access, avoiding "allow always", requiring confirmation before sending, sharing, paying or contracting, and limiting what an agent can do if hidden instructions in emails, websites or documents deceive it. At work, staff should use only organisation-approved assistants and not connect professional email or documents to personal AI accounts.

The agency stresses that **responsibility cannot rest solely with users**: providers should offer permissions distinguishing viewing from acting, configurable confirmations, an action history, an immediate stop function and memory deletion.

The Dutch data protection authority (AP) has published its first 'Staat van de gegevensbescherming bij de Belastingdienst', reviewing the Tax Administration under the intensified supervision it began in 2024. It assessed compliance and pace of improvement across **12 topics**.

The Belastingdienst now meets the law on two: reporting data breaches and carrying out DPIAs. On the other ten it falls short. According to the AP, its selection algorithms are insufficiently justified to prevent discrimination, it cannot yet show human involvement in algorithmic decisions where the law requires it, personal data is not adequately secured, and its internal privacy organisation needs to work better. The citizen service number (BSN) is due to be removed from payment references for citizens at the end of 2026.

The AP's instructions are legally binding and it can impose sanctions if they are not followed. Intensified supervision runs **for now until mid-2029**, with the next report due in January 2028.

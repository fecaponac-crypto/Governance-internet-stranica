Norway's data protection authority, Datatilsynet, has published a statement supporting the Consumer Authority's (Forbrukertilsynet) campaign setting out **seven rules for children's consumer protection**. Director Line Coll said that much online advertising aimed at children and young people depends on extensive collection and use of personal data, including viewing time, searches, location and social connections, which large technology companies use to build profiles that decide which ads children see.

Coll said the GDPR gives children particular protection, especially for marketing and profiling, and that this applies even when children are not the primary target audience. In Norway, young people can consent to processing from **age 13**, but consent must be freely given, informed and unambiguous; a tap on an arbitrary "accept" button is not enough.

The authority highlighted disguised advertising and competitions that encourage sharing as rules with direct privacy implications.

Jacek Szczytko, an associate at the law firm Dudkowiak Putyra, argues in an IAPP piece that EU lawmakers have given the **data protection impact assessment** an unusual role in the Payment Services Regulation (PSR) regime for sharing fraud data between payment service providers. The piece's title describes the DPIA as the 'ticket of entry' to that sharing.

The package, made up of the PSR and the third Payment Services Directive (PSD3), follows a provisional Council–Parliament agreement of 27 November 2025. The Council confirmed final compromise texts in April 2026 and adopted its first-reading position in late October 2026. The author contrasts the regime with the Commission's 2023 proposal, which covered transaction monitoring and data sharing together in Article 83.

According to the author, Parliament's second-reading vote was planned for December 2026, with **most rules applying 21 months after entry into force**, likely in late 2028.

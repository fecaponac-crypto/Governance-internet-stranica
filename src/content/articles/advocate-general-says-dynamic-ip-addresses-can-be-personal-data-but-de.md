On 5 November 2026, Advocate General Maciej Szpunar delivered his Opinion in **Case C-654/25**, a German reference on a website that sent visitors' dynamic IP addresses to Google in the US through a default Google Fonts configuration. According to the German Federal Court of Justice, the claimant used automated tools to trigger such visits more than 100,000 times and then sought compensation.

The AG said a dynamic IP address can be personal data for a recipient with means reasonably likely to identify the person, and indirectly for the website operator if it knew or should have known of those means. On Article 82 GDPR, he said **no non-material damage arises where someone knowingly provokes infringements in an automated, repeated way** to obtain compensation. Abuse of rights needs an objective and a subjective element; financial gain need only be the primary aim.

The Opinion is not binding. The CJEU will now deliberate.

The European Commission has **preliminarily found TikTok in breach of the Digital Services Act** over the "rabbit hole effect" of its 'For You' feed, it announced on 6 November 2026. The more a user watches or likes a type of content, the more similar content the algorithm recommends. When that content concerns sadness, isolation or body image, the Commission says, it can harm users' mental health, especially that of vulnerable adults and minors.

The Commission found that TikTok did not adequately assess this risk and that its mitigation measures are not effective. Measures to diversify content do not sufficiently reduce repeated recommendations, and controls such as the "not interested" button may not change the feed, because the algorithm prioritises viewing time over users' choices. TikTok should rely more on users' **explicit preferences**, stop using viewing time as an indicator for recommendations, make user controls easier to use and regularly test its algorithm.

The findings are preliminary: TikTok can examine the file and reply in writing, and the European Board for Digital Services will be consulted. A non-compliance decision could bring a fine of up to **6% of worldwide annual turnover**. The same proceedings produced preliminary findings on addictive design in February 2026 and on the protection of minors in July.

EPIC and Professor Seema Patel, represented by Harvard Law School's Cyberlaw Clinic, have filed an amicus brief in Spirit Airlines' bankruptcy proceedings opposing the proposed sale of employee data to Google for use in AI training. According to EPIC, the airline proposes to sell the data for **$10 million**. It includes payroll, training, discipline, travel and internal communications records, some going back decades.

The brief supports a limited objection filed by the Association of Flight Attendants-CWA, AFL-CIO. EPIC argues that the sale agreement clearly protects only consumer data, not employee data. It also says **deidentification is "not a magic wand"** because employees could still be identified through inferences drawn from the combined dataset.

EPIC further warns that such sales would encourage employers to over-collect worker data. The brief asks the court to require stronger privacy protections. The sources do not say when the court will rule.

*Editor's note: This article relies solely on an announcement by EPIC, an advocacy group that is a party to the filing.*

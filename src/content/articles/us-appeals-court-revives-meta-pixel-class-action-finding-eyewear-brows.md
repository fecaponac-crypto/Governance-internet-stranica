The US Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit has revived a putative class action alleging that optical retailer Eyemart Express sent customers' health-related website activity to Meta through the Meta Pixel without consent. In a published decision of **23 October 2026**, the court reversed the dismissal and remanded the case.

The court found the plaintiffs had standing, likening unauthorised disclosure of health information to traditional privacy torts. It held that searching for prescription eyewear and scheduling an eye exam could plausibly be **individually identifiable health information** under HIPAA's definition, even without a diagnosis or prescription. Plaintiffs also alleged Eyemart uploaded customer lists with emails and eyewear viewed so Meta could match profiles.

The ruling is at the pleading stage. According to Privacy World, it does not settle whether Eyemart broke any law; the district court must now assess the remaining elements of the claims.

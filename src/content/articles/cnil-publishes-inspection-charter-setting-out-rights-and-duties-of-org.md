France's data protection authority, the CNIL, has published a **charter of inspections**. It aims to make its investigative work more transparent and help inspections run smoothly.

The charter sets out, as precisely as possible, the rights and obligations of organisations under inspection, with particular reference to the GDPR and the French Data Protection Act (loi Informatique et Libertés). It also explains how an inspection unfolds and what may follow, whatever form it takes, along with principles of good conduct.

According to the CNIL, inspections can take place on site, at its premises following a summons, online or on the basis of documents. It reported **300 inspections in 2019**, of which 53 were online and 45 were document-based. Complaints prompted 43% of them, but the CNIL can also act on its own initiative, for example in response to current events. Each year it also sets priority themes for future inspections.

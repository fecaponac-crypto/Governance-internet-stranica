The European Commission has released its proposal for the **Digital Fairness Act (DFA)**, part of a wider package on online consumer protection. In an opinion piece for the IAPP, Isabelle Roccia, its Managing Director for Europe, notes that the text updates some rules shaped around 20 years ago. Because it is a regulation, it would apply directly in all 27 member states.

According to Roccia, the Commission estimates the proposal could yield up to **EUR 13.7 billion a year in consumer savings**. She writes that observers could legitimately wonder what legislative gaps the DFA aims to fill, given that existing consumer, data protection, platform and AI rules already cross paths with it.

Her main takeaway is that measures on addictive design, overspending, personalised advertising and dark patterns could prompt substantial design changes. The proposal will now be negotiated with member states and the European Parliament, and the text is expected to change.

*Editor's note: This article relies on a contributed opinion piece published by the IAPP rather than on the Commission's proposal itself.*

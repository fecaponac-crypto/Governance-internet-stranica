The Investigatory Powers Tribunal ruled on **19 November 2026** that confirming the Home Secretary had given Apple a technical capability notice (TCN) under section 253 of the Investigatory Powers Act 2016 would not damage national security. Three judges heard claims from Apple and from Privacy International, Liberty and two individuals.

The tribunal inferred that briefings confirming the notice to The Times and the Financial Times had taken place and were authorised by ministers, senior officials or special advisers. It also found that letting Apple brief US officials without assurances of confidentiality was a further departure from the policy. It gave the Home Office's leak investigations "little or no evidential value".

Apple has **five grounds of challenge**. The first two, on statutory interpretation, can be decided entirely in open court. Whether the notice's details can be disclosed will be decided later. The parties expect a substantive hearing in the first half of 2027.

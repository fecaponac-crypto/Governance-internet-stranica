A federal judge in Washington DC has **granted a preliminary injunction and stayed the "Voter Data Transmission Policy"** of the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and the Department of Justice (DOJ). Under the policy, the agencies sent states and localities lists of registered voters they claimed were likely noncitizens, asking officials to act on them. Judge Tanya S. Chutkan ruled on 2 November 2026, the day before the midterm elections, on a motion by LULAC and EPIC.

The court found the plaintiffs likely to succeed on their claims that **no statute authorises the policy**, and that it violates the **Privacy Act**. By amassing millions of voter records and enhancing them with third-party and other federal data, the agencies had likely created new systems of records without publishing the required notice. Their bulk disclosures did not fit the Act's law enforcement exception.

The opinion cites DOJ's transfer of data on more than 20,000 people to Los Angeles County and a list sent to Washington state. The injunction does not decide the lawsuit, which continues.

*Editor's note: This article is based on the court's memorandum opinion; the separate order setting out the injunction's terms was not reviewed.*

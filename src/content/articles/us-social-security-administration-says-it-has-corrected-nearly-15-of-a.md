The US Social Security Administration (SSA) says it has corrected **nearly 15% of active Social Security Numbers**, across 330 million citizens and nearly 390 million total records, according to Nextgov/FCW's report of an agency press release. SSA links the work to fraud prevention and programme administration.

According to SSA, the work added 17 million missing death dates using internal SSA and State Department data. It also updated about 25 million records lacking citizenship information using the Department of Homeland Security's SAVE service. **A further 8.5 million records with inaccurate immigration status** are still being corrected.

The National Committee to Preserve Social Security and Medicare, a nonprofit, called SAVE "incomplete and prone to errors" in an 23 October blog post. It said the service has wrongly flagged citizens as non-citizens, and warned that those inaccuracies could pass into SSA files. SSA referred questions to its release.

*Editor's note: This article relies on secondary news reports of an SSA press release that was not reviewed directly, and on criticism from an advocacy group.*

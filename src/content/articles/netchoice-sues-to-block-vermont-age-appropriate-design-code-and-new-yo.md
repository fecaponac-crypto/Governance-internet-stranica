NetChoice has filed lawsuits challenging the constitutionality of two state laws on children's online safety: one in the US District Court for the Southern District of New York on **23 November 2026**, and one in the US District Court for the District of Vermont, where its case page dates the complaint to 24 November. The Vermont Age-Appropriate Design Code imposes privacy and safe design requirements on tech companies. New York's SAFE for Kids Act limits addictive feeds and night-time notifications for minors.

The Electronic Privacy Information Center (EPIC), which supported the Vermont law, argues that NetChoice is recasting content-neutral data and design rules as limits on content. It says both laws target the use of personal data to profile users behaviourally in feeds, not the content moderation choices the Supreme Court protected in Moody v. NetChoice. EPIC also says earlier industry wins were narrow. One example is the Ninth Circuit's 2024 ruling in X Corp. v. Bonta, which blocked California rules requiring reports on how platforms define state-specified content categories.

No court has ruled on the new suits.

*Editor's note: The filing details come from NetChoice's own case pages; the arguments against the suits come from EPIC, an advocacy group.*

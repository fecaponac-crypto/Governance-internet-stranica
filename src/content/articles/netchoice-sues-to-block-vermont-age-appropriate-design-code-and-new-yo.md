NetChoice filed lawsuits on **24 November 2026** challenging the constitutionality of two state laws on children's online safety, according to the Electronic Privacy Information Center (EPIC). The Vermont Age-Appropriate Design Code imposes privacy and safe design requirements on tech companies. New York's SAFE for Kids Act limits addictive feeds and night-time notifications for minors.

EPIC argues that NetChoice is recasting content-neutral data and design rules as limits on content. It says both laws target the use of personal data to profile users behaviourally in feeds, not the content moderation choices the Supreme Court protected in Moody v. NetChoice. EPIC also says earlier industry wins were narrow. One example is the Ninth Circuit's 2024 ruling in X Corp. v. Bonta, which blocked California rules requiring reports on how platforms define state-specified content categories.

No court has ruled on the new suits.

*Editor's note: The account of the new lawsuits comes only from EPIC, an advocacy group that supported the Vermont law; the court opinion cited is earlier background.*

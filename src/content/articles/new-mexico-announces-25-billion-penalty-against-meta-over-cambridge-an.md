The New Mexico Department of Justice has announced that the state is imposing a **$25 billion penalty** on Meta for breaching New Mexico's Unfair Practices Act, according to the Electronic Privacy Information Center (EPIC). A jury found that Meta committed **43,899,725 violations** of the Act during the Cambridge Analytica scandal.

The jury examined Meta's statements and practices on third-party access to personal data, user privacy violations and its handling of harmful content.

The penalty adds to earlier Cambridge Analytica-related costs. In 2022, Meta agreed to pay $725 million to settle a class action over the mishandling of 87 million users' data. In August, it settled several Cambridge Analytica privacy claims as part of a settlement of nearly $17 billion with a coalition of state attorneys general. EPIC reports that $459 million of that sum relates to Cambridge Analytica claims. The source does not say whether Meta will appeal.

*Editor's note: This article is based on a secondary report by EPIC, a privacy advocacy group, not on the New Mexico Department of Justice's own announcement.*

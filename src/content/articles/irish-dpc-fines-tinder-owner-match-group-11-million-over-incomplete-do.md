Ireland's Data Protection Commission (DPC) announced on 13 November 2026 a **€11 million fine** and a reprimand for MTCH Technology Services Limited, which runs Tinder in the EU, over how it answered users' requests for a copy of their personal data under **Article 15(3) GDPR**.

The DPC opened the own-volition inquiry in January 2020 as lead supervisory authority. It examined Tinder's self-service "Download My Data" tool, which was presented to users as a way to obtain a copy of their Tinder data. The DPC found that the tool **did not provide all of their personal data**: certain categories were left out **without notice to the user**, or provided in summary form only, and the data it did provide did not meet the requirements of the right of access.

Deputy Commissioner Graham Doyle said tens of thousands of users used the tool during the period examined. He said such automated tools "do not need to provide a complete copy of personal data but they must be fair and transparent to individuals regarding the extent of the data provided."

Besides the fine, the DPC ordered MTCH to bring its processing into compliance **within three months**. The decision was taken by Commissioners Des Hogan, Dale Sunderland and Niamh Sweeney, and the DPC says it will publish the full decision in due course.

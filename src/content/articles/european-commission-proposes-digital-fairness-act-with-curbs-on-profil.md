The European Commission adopted its proposal for a **Digital Fairness Act (DFA)** on 11 November 2026. It forms part of a Consumer Package that also contains a Simplification Directive amending the Consumer Rights and ePrivacy Directives, and a revised Consumer Protection Cooperation (CPC) Regulation. According to Covington, the DFA would replace the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive with a directly applicable regulation.

New rules would cover dark patterns, influencer marketing, addictive design, loot boxes, subscriptions and AI shopping agents. Profiling-based advertising using the data of consumers known with reasonable certainty to be minors, or GDPR special-category data, would be banned. So would profiling-based pricing that is reasonably likely to harm minors or vulnerable consumers.

The Commission could enforce widespread EU-wide infringements directly, with fines of **up to 6% of worldwide annual turnover**. Parliament and Council must still agree a final text. The DFA would apply 12 months after entry into force.

European Digital Rights (EDRi) has published a position paper on data retention, which it describes as the legal requirement for internet service providers to keep users' metadata **solely so that law enforcement and intelligence services can access it later**, beyond what is needed for billing or network security.

EDRi argues that metadata such as numbers called, call times, websites visited and device location is more revealing than content because it is highly structured and easy to analyse. It points to the CJEU's 2014 finding that mass retention of traffic and location data was contrary to the EU Charter, and to harms including a breach at AT&T that, according to EDRi, exposed call and text logs of more than 100 million customers.

The group calls for confidentiality of communications to remain the main rule and for **only targeted retention via quick freeze orders**, with safeguards such as prior judicial authorisation, in any new EU instrument.

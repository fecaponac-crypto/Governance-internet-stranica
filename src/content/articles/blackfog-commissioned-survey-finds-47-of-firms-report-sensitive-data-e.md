Cybersecurity vendor BlackFog has published research, carried out by Sapio Research in September 2026, surveying 400 IT security decision-makers at US and UK companies with more than 500 employees. **47% said their organisation had experienced an incident in which sensitive data was exposed through an AI tool**, and 77% of those said a company-approved tool was involved.

The survey points to visibility gaps: 62% could identify users or departments sending data to AI tools, but only 48% could provide any record of the types of data that left through them. Although 98% reported an AI usage policy, 35% relied on written prompt rules without technical enforcement. **56% said autonomous AI agents acting for employees fall outside existing controls**, rising to 70% in technology and telecoms.

According to CDO Magazine, the survey does not identify the organisations, tools or timing of incidents, nor whether exposed data reached third parties.

*Editor's note: This article relies on a vendor-commissioned survey announced by BlackFog and on trade press coverage of it; the findings have not been independently verified.*

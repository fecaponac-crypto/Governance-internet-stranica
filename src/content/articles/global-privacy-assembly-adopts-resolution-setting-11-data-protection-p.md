The Global Privacy Assembly, the worldwide network of data protection authorities, adopted a resolution on artificial intelligence in education in **November 2026**, according to France's CNIL, which chairs the assembly's Digital Education Working Group.

The resolution sets out **11 principles** for AI used with children in schools. They include the child's best interests as the cardinal principle, defining roles and responsibilities in advance, a legal basis and strict purpose limitation, and impact assessments. Further principles cover privacy by design and default, data accuracy and limited retention, safeguards on automated decision-making, security, accountability, procurement and processing agreements, and the rights of children, guardians and staff.

The authorities also made six commitments. These include developing complementary guidance and calling for a legal framework for AI in education. The annexes include AI literacy guidance and a model AI charter for schools.

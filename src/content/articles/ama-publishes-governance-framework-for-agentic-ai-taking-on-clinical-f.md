The American Medical Association (AMA) has published a governance framework for agentic AI systems that assume clinical functions. According to Robinson+Cole, it was released on 13 October. Its starting principle is that clinical authority should be **"earned through evidence and maintained through demonstrated performance"**, with authority and oversight able to change as evidence, performance, risk or the technology evolve.

The framework is built around **four connected questions**: what clinical function the AI performs; what evidence shows it can do so and what happens if it fails; what it should be permitted to do and under which conditions; and who is accountable for risks and outcomes. According to Robinson+Cole, accountability is to be assigned by each participant's authority, control, risk knowledge and ability to prevent harm.

The guidance is not binding. The AMA says the questions will shape its work with physicians and others on liability, licensure and regulation.

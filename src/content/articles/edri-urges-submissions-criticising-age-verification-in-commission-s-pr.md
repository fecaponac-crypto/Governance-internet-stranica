European Digital Rights (EDRi) has called on readers to respond to the European Commission's public consultation on its proposed **Kids Act**, a regulation intended to improve young people's safety online. According to EDRi, the consultation is open **until midnight on 3 December**.

EDRi says it is still finalising its position. It argues that widespread mandatory age verification treats everyone as a suspected child and will unfairly restrict people who lack ID documents, equipment, digital literacy or trust. It contends that social media bans should be a last resort, applied only if the provider can demonstrate it has not managed to mitigate the risks to young people, and limited to the relevant part of a service.

The network also wants adults to receive the same protections as minors, covering designs fostering "excessive or compulsive use" (Article 9), recommender optimisation that exploits user vulnerability (Article 10(2)) and tracking-based "implicit engagement-based signals". It calls the proof of parental authority (Article 26) too extreme and guardians' powers over child accounts overly intrusive.

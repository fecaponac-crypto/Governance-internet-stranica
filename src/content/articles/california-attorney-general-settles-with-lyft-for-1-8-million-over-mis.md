California Attorney General Rob Bonta announced a settlement with Lyft, Inc. on 29 October 2026, resolving allegations that it breached the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) and the state's Unfair Competition Law. Lyft agreed to pay **$1.8 million in civil penalties**.

According to the Attorney General, Lyft had been selling or sharing users' personal information, including IP addresses and non-precise geolocation, to target ads in its app since mid-2023. Yet it offered **no in-app opt-out**. Users had to follow several links from the in-app privacy policy to a "Do Not Sell or Share" webpage, log in again and submit more personal information. The office called this an "illegal and unnecessary barrier".

Lyft must now add an in-app opt-out and notify Californians of their right to opt out of sale. The case arose from a March 2025 sweep on geolocation data and is the office's ninth CCPA enforcement action.

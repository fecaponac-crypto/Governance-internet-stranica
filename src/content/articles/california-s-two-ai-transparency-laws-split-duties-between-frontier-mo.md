California now has two AI transparency statutes in force, both from **1 January 2026**. SB 53, the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act signed by Governor Gavin Newsom in September 2025, requires large frontier developers to publish a framework showing how they have incorporated national and international standards and industry best practice. It also creates a route for reporting critical safety incidents to the Office of Emergency Services, protects whistleblowers and sets a civil penalty enforceable by the Attorney General.

According to an IAPP analysis, SB 53 applies only to developers above a 10^26 computing threshold, with large frontier developers also exceeding USD 500 million in annual revenue. AB 2013, the Training Data Transparency Act (TDTA), reaches far wider: any developer that designs or **substantially modifies** a generative AI system for public use, a term that includes retraining or fine-tuning.

California has enacted AB 1979, which brings consumer-facing health care chatbots within the state's Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA), according to a law firm summary. Any business offering a health care chatbot so consumers can manage their information, or for diagnosis, treatment or management of a condition, is **deemed a provider of health care** and faces the same standards and penalties.

A "health care chatbot" is a generative AI system with a natural language interface that gives adaptive, human-like responses, is marketed as supporting health services and uses chatbot information to do so.

The Act also requires health facilities and practices to take reasonable steps so licensed clinicians can exercise independent judgment when care is informed by a "clinical decision support system". It bars using AI to direct unlicensed staff in, or independently perform, licensed clinical functions; supervised trainees and routine documentation tools are exempt. The law **takes effect on 1 January 2027**.

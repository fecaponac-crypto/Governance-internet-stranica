The European Commission adopted its proposal for a **Digital Fairness Act** (COM(2026) 1100) on 11 November 2026. It would repeal the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive and replace it with a directly applicable regulation, ending 27 national transposition regimes. It would also allow the Commission to enforce directly in specific cases under a parallel revision of the Consumer Protection Cooperation Regulation.

The new rules cover dark patterns, influencer marketing, addictive design, virtual currencies, loot boxes, pricing and subscriptions. Personalised advertising aimed at minors, or based on GDPR special categories of data, would be prohibited. Personalised pricing towards minors or vulnerable consumers would also be banned where it leads to less favourable results. The burden of proof would be eased in technologically complex cases.

According to the law firm Squire Patton Boggs, authorities could order businesses to disclose evidence on a practice's design and behavioural impact. The firm says the rules are expected to apply **12 months after entry into force**. The text now enters the ordinary legislative procedure.

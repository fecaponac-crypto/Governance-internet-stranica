In a published opinion of **9 October 2026**, a unanimous Ninth Circuit panel held in *Black v. IEC Group, Inc.* that health plan participants had Article III standing after their claims administrator, AmeriBen, emailed a spreadsheet of their health claims data to one or more plan members. The District of Idaho had dismissed the case for lack of a likely future injury.

The panel found a close historical analogue in the action for **breach of confidence**: the injury lies in the unconsented disclosure itself, without any need to show identity theft or fraud. It also relied on contract law and on HIPAA's protection of data such as provider names, dates of service and amounts billed.

According to Privacy World, plaintiffs are likely to extend the theory to pixel and cookie tracking cases. The ruling deepens a split with the Third, Fourth and Seventh Circuits, which the blog says may lead to a Supreme Court review.

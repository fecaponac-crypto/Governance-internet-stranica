The Netherlands' Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (AP) has advised consumers not to buy AI camera glasses for general use. Its decision follows its own study of **six models available on the Dutch market**, two of which were tested in detail in simulated real-life situations.

In one simulated emergency, a pair of glasses repeatedly said an ambulance was on its way when no emergency services had been called. The glasses also gave dating advice to an adult who said they were interested in a minor. They made errors reading medicine leaflets and severe weather warnings.

On privacy, the AP found that recording lights were hard to see and easy to cover. **Most glasses kept filming once the light was taped over**, even though manufacturers said this was not possible. The AI functions process data on external servers, often outside the EU. The AP also found security flaws that could let someone nearby connect to the glasses, view recordings or operate the camera. It has called on manufacturers to show compliance with EU rules, including the AI Act, and has urged Dutch and European policymakers to take its findings into account.

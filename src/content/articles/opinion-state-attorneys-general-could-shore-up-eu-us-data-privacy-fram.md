In a contributed IAPP opinion piece, Hogan Lovells lawyers Bret Cohen and Julian Flamant respond to the US Supreme Court's ruling in Trump v. Slaughter of 29 June 2026, which overruled Humphrey's Executor and held that FTC commissioners are subject to the president's power of removal. **Recital 60 of the Commission's 2023 DPF adequacy decision** relied on that limit, which allowed removal only for inefficiency, neglect of duty or malfeasance.

On 31 July the EDPB asked the Commission to assess whether the judgment affects the adequacy decision. They add that Max Schrems has urged the Commission to "orderly withdraw" from the framework. Their own view is that the FTC keeps its enforcement powers.

If further safeguards are needed, they propose that **a limited group of qualified state attorneys general** serve as supplemental enforcers. These offices would draw on state consumer protection laws and could be named by organisations when they self-certify. The authors acknowledge open questions on differing powers, jurisdiction over EU complaints and coordination between offices.

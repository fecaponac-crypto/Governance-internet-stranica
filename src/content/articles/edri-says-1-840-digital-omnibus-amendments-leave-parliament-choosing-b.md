European Digital Rights (EDRi) has analysed the data protection and privacy amendments tabled in the European Parliament on the Digital Omnibus on Data. The Commission proposal, presented in November 2025, would amend the GDPR, ePrivacy and other EU data rules. According to EDRi, MEPs tabled 1,762 amendments on top of 78 in the joint LIBE-ITRE draft report, for a total of **1,840 amendments**.

EDRi groups them into three directions: restoring safeguards, improving parts of the proposal, and creating new exclusions or permissions. It sees the sharpest disputes over a holder-dependent definition of personal data and a dedicated provision treating AI development and operation as a legitimate-interest context. It welcomes binding browser- or device-level privacy signals and shared DPIA tools.

EDRi says the Omnibus should be rejected as a whole, noting that the **Council agreed its position on 21 October 2026**. Parliament is still considering the amendments.

*Editor's note: This article relies solely on an analysis by EDRi, an advocacy group that opposes the Digital Omnibus.*

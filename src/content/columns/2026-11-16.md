The week's most consequential development is Italy's Garante [fining Meta €805 million](/news/italy-s-garante-fines-meta-805-million-over-facebook-app-s-access-to-a/) over the Facebook app for Android. Its "suggestions from your gallery" feature read photos, videos and metadata on Italian users' phones without specific consent. The size of the fine matters less to practitioners than the two legal points behind it.

First, the Garante decided the case under Article 122 of the Italian Privacy Code, which transposes the ePrivacy directive. Under that provision, any access to information on a smartphone needs free and informed consent, whether or not data leaves the device and whether or not anyone is harmed. Second, it rejected Meta's argument that Ireland's DPC should lead, because ePrivacy has no one-stop-shop.

My read: any organisation with an EU app should assume that device access, including storage, galleries and sensors, can be pursued by any national authority, whatever its GDPR lead authority. The specific failure is also common. Meta reused one app-wide permission behind a generic prompt for a secondary feature that was switched on by default. Meta now has 60 days to obtain separate consent and turn the feature off by default, including for users who had already granted permission. The press release does not say whether Meta will appeal.

## Defaults and sequencing are now the test

I think the thread through this week is that regulators are looking at the order in which things happen and at default settings, not only at whether a choice exists.

- California's CalPrivacy [advanced draft amendments](/news/calprivacy-advances-ccpa-amendments-requiring-opt-out-signals-to-be-ho/) stating that a business whose advertising cookies or pixels fire before its tag tools check for a GPC signal has not complied. The opt-out must also reach pseudonymous profiles linked through matching techniques. That makes it a problem of identity resolution and lineage, which master data teams will recognise. A 45-day comment period is next; no date has been given.
- Mexico's [presidential decree](/news/mexico-decree-requires-platforms-to-verify-age-set-strict-defaults-and/) requires new accounts to start with the strictest privacy and safety settings, and requires age checks that go beyond self-declaration. Platforms must document and report their compliance within 30 calendar days of entry into force. According to IAPP, the decree names no supervisory authority and sets no sanctions.
- The European Commission's [Digital Fairness Act proposal](/news/european-commission-proposes-digital-fairness-act-banning-dark-pattern/) would ban business-to-consumer dark patterns and prohibit personalised advertising aimed at minors or based on GDPR special-category data. It is a proposal, and Parliament and Council have yet to negotiate it. Even so, it makes sensitive-attribute and age tagging an input to marketing eligibility.

## What an access request has to return

The Irish DPC [fined Tinder's EU operator €11 million](/news/irish-dpc-fines-tinder-owner-match-group-11-million-over-incomplete-do/) over its "Download My Data" tool, which left out some categories of data without telling users. The inquiry opened in January 2020. The DPC did not say a self-service export must contain everything. Deputy Commissioner Graham Doyle said such tools "must be fair and transparent to individuals regarding the extent of the data provided." The control to check is a reconciliation between what the export returns and what your data inventory says you hold.

The same question is coming for HR systems. The EDPB has [chosen automated decision-making in recruitment](/news/edpb-picks-automated-decision-making-in-recruitment-as-2027-coordinate/) as its 2027 coordinated enforcement topic, covering Article 22 and the right of access. Authorities take part voluntarily, but those that opt in will use a shared questionnaire. Teams should know where candidate screening is solely automated and whether they can explain its logic when a candidate asks.

## Dependencies you do not control

Two stories show the risk of relying on a single platform. Norway's Datatilsynet has [received breach notifications](/news/norway-s-datatilsynet-receives-breach-notifications-over-amadeus-trave/) from Berg-Hansen and the Norwegian group over the Amadeus compromise. According to Norway's NSM, a threat actor had access to Amadeus systems for at least three years. The breach happened at the supplier, but the notification duties fell on each organisation whose data was there.

Microsoft is [retiring Purview Data Governance](/news/microsoft-to-retire-purview-data-governance-ending-support-on-12-novem/), with support ending on 12 November 2027. Governance moves to the OneLake catalog in Fabric, while labelling and data loss prevention stay in Purview. Microsoft itself says some organisations need more than Fabric offers today. Glossaries, curated descriptions and approval workflows cannot be rediscovered by scanning source systems, so the migration plan needs a named owner now. This also sits uneasily with [Gartner's survey](/news/gartner-survey-71-of-organisations-expect-to-deploy-agentic-ai-within/), which found that 71% of organisations expect to deploy agentic AI within a year but only 21% have governance in place. ## The week in numbers

We published 31 stories and recorded six enforcement steps. The stated amounts were EUR 816,000,000 and NOK 10,000. The euro figure is the Meta and Tinder fines together; the Norwegian figure is a [fine on a private individual](/news/norway-s-datatilsynet-fines-individual-nok-10-000-for-private-credit-c/). Separately, the CNIL [issued ten fines of up to EUR 20,000](/news/cnil-issues-ten-fines-of-up-to-eur-20-000-to-local-authorities-and-a-p/) to local authorities and a public body over cybersecurity.

## Chile: do not move your date yet

The [bill to postpone Law 21.719](/news/chilean-bill-to-delay-data-protection-law-to-15-october-2027-passes-se/) to 15 October 2027 has passed the Senate and is now before the Chamber's Finance Committee. Until it is enacted, 1 December 2026 remains the legal date, and that is two weeks away.

## What I'm watching
